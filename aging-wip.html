Aging WIP

Catch at-risk work before it slips — not after.

Aging WIP is the proactive companion to the Scatterplot. The Scatterplot shows finished work; this shows your currently open work and how long it's been aging, so you can act before an item becomes an outlier.

Reading a dot

  • X (column) — the item's current Doing status (its lane in the workflow).
  • Y (vertical) — its age: days since it first entered a Doing status (the commitment point), up to now.
  • Click a dot, or a row in "Needs attention," to open its status history and rework. The issue key opens it directly in Jira.

The percentile lines are the same SLEs

The dashed 50/70/85/95th lines come from your completed cycle times — the same lines shown on the Scatterplot. An open item sitting above the 85th line has already aged longer than 85% of finished items took end-to-end, so it's statistically likely to become a slow outlier. Above the 95th line, it's critical.

Rework: returns & reopens

A return is a backward step in the workflow that didn't come from Done (e.g. QA → In Development). A reopen is a backward step from a Done status. "Backward" follows your Status Mapping order, so it even catches bounce-backs within the Doing lanes. High rework on an aging item is a strong "needs a closer look" signal — it usually means the item is churning rather than progressing.

How to use it

  • It's a "right now" snapshot, not history — best used in daily stand-ups or WIP reviews.
  • Work the "Needs attention" table top-down: oldest and highest-risk first. Pull these to done before starting new work.
  • Items aging in a queue or hand-off status (Ready for…, Blocked) are the usual culprits — they erode flow efficiency.
  • Rising age across a column over time is a sign to apply or tighten a WIP limit.

Age starts at the first Doing transition — or at the issue's creation date, if it was created directly into a Doing status.
